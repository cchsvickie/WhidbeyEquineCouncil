Trail Safety

Trail safety for horseback riding on Whidbey Island focuses on agency-specific rules, multi-use trail etiquette, personal/horse preparation, and Leave No Trace principles. Always verify current conditions, closures, and designated equestrian trails directly with the managing agency before your ride, as rules can change due to weather, resource protection, or safety issues.

Trail Etiquette & Right of Way

Washington’s trails are often shared with hikers, runners, bicyclists, and motorized users. BCHW’s “3S” rule (Stop – Stand – Speak) helps prevent spooking horses (prey animals whose instinct is flight):

For non-riders encountering horses:

  • STOP well in advance.
  • STAND downhill (anything above a horse’s head can look like a predator).
  • SPEAK calmly (“Hello”) and ask the rider what to do. Yield to the rider’s instructions.

For riders:

  • Ensure your horse is trained and seasoned for trails, obstacles, other users, dogs, packs, etc.
  • Safety first – dismount if needed.
  • Pull aside (preferably downhill) for others; communicate clearly.
  • Keep dogs under control and follow leash rules.
  • Scoop poop at staging areas/trailheads and kick it off the trail.
  • Ride single-file where appropriate; go slow in poor visibility.

Horses have the right-of-way on most shared trails in WA public lands.

Sources: pbchw.org • dnr.wa.gov

Trail Preparedness

Emergency Procedures & First Aid

Carry a charged phone, first aid kit, and hoof boot. Know the location of the nearest trailhead. Have a plan for getting help if your horse is injured or you are hurt.

Wildlife Encounters

Stay alert for deer, coyotes, and occasional black bears. Keep your horse calm and give wildlife plenty of space. Never approach or feed wild animals.

Weather & Trail Conditions

Check the forecast before riding. Avoid muddy or flooded trails after heavy rain. Be prepared for sudden wind or fog common on Whidbey Island.

Personal and Horse Safety

  • Wear a properly fitted ASTM/SEI-certified equestrian helmet (replace after impact). This is a universal recommendation.
  • Ride a fit, calm horse experienced with the terrain. Use appropriate tack and consider protective boots for rocky/muddy WA trails.
  • Prepare for Washington conditions: Rain makes trails slippery (mud, roots, rocks). Check weather, carry layers/rain gear, and know your route. Be alert for wildlife (bears in some areas), changing conditions, and working forest activity.
  • Carry water, first-aid (for you and horse), map/compass or GPS, and a charged phone. Tell someone your plans.
  • Group riding: Experienced riders/horses lead; control spacing to avoid bunching or kicking.
  • Leave No Trace (BCHW principles): Plan ahead, stay on trails, pack out manure/trash where required, use highlines or hobbles to minimize trampling, scatter manure off-trail, and avoid damaging vegetation. In backcountry, check grazing limits and use certified weed-free feed.

Source: bchw.org
