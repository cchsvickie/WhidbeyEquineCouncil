Trail Safety

Trail safety for horseback riding on Washington state’s public parks and lands focuses on agency-specific rules (primarily Washington State Parks and DNR-managed state trust lands), multi-use trail etiquette, personal/horse preparation, and Leave No Trace principles. Always verify current conditions, closures, and designated equestrian trails directly with the managing agency before your ride, as rules can change due to weather, resource protection, or safety issues. parks.wa.gov    dnr.wa.gov

Key Resources

  • Washington State Parks: parks.wa.gov (rules, trail finder, horseback riding page)
  • DNR Recreation: dnr.wa.gov/recreation (recreation guide PDF, horse camps, maps)
  • Back Country Horsemen of Washington (BCHW): bchw.org (trail safety, 3S, backcountry ethics booklet)
  • U.S. Forest Service (WA forests): fs.usda.gov (search by forest for equestrian info)
  • Local chapters of BCHW or trail groups for area-specific advice.

Trail Etiquette & Right of Way

Yield to hikers and bikers. Announce your presence when approaching from behind. Ride on the right side of the trail when possible. Pass only when safe and with clear communication.

Emergency Procedures & First Aid

Carry a charged phone, first aid kit, and hoof boot. Know the location of the nearest trailhead. Have a plan for getting help if your horse is injured or you are hurt.

Wildlife Encounters

Stay alert for deer, coyotes, and occasional black bears. Keep your horse calm and give wildlife plenty of space. Never approach or feed wild animals.

Weather & Trail Conditions

Check the forecast before riding. Avoid muddy or flooded trails after heavy rain. Be prepared for sudden wind or fog common on Whidbey Island.

Gear Checklist

Helmet, sturdy boots, long pants, water, phone, map, hoof pick, lead rope, and a basic first aid kit for both you and your horse.
